Section 02

Data

Where every number in this project comes from, what was thrown away before analysis, and how someone else could obtain exactly the same files.

Five cohorts, four platforms

TCGA-BRCA is the primary cohort: it supplies the expression matrix, the clinical and follow-up data, the somatic mutation calls and the PAM50 subtype calls. The four GEO series do one job only — testing whether a TCGA finding survives a change of platform and population.

Why these four, and not the four largest. Platform diversity beats raw sample count. A gene confirmed on Affymetrix and Agilent and an independent RNA-seq cohort is unlikely to be a probe artefact; the same gene confirmed across three studies on one array platform could still be one. These four give three platforms, three continents of ancestry, and both paired and unpaired designs. Around thirty other GEO series were surveyed and rejected — for tumour-only designs, unusable annotation, or normal arms too small to carry any power.

Two handling decisions worth stating. GSE58135's 28 breast cancer cell lines are excluded at download, because this is a comparison of tumour against normal tissue. GSE86374's groups are taken from its tissue description rather than its molecular-subtype field, in which 14 of the tumours are labelled “Normal-like” — reading groups off that field would have put tumours in the normal arm. GSE70947 is also the second cohort of a larger study, so its companion cohort cannot later be counted as further replication.

TCGA-BRCA in detail

The expression matrix holds 60,660 Ensembl genes across 1,224 aliquots — 1,111 primary tumours and 113 solid-tissue normals. Those tumours come from 1,095 unique patients, because 16 patients contributed more than one aliquot, and all 113 normals come from patients who also contributed a tumour, which is what makes the matched-pair figure possible.

757 white · 183 Black or African American · 61 Asian · 1 American Indian or Alaska Native · 95 not reported. The cohort skews heavily white, which is a limit on how far any result here generalises. The gender column exists but holds no values, so no sex breakdown is quoted anywhere on this site.

PAM50 subtypes, 1,083 of 1,095

Luminal A 562 (51.9%) · Luminal B 209 (19.3%) · Basal-like 190 (17.5%) · HER2-enriched 82 (7.6%) · Normal-like 40 (3.7%). Twelve patients have no call and are dropped rather than shown as a sixth group.

Somatic mutations

89,568 masked somatic mutation calls across 991 samples. Fewer samples carry mutation calls than expression, so the mutation cohort is a subset and must never be described with the expression n.

Normal-like is not a fifth disease. It is widely read as a low-tumour-purity artefact — a sample with too little tumour in it to classify. It stays visible and labelled in every figure on this site, but it is never presented as a biological subtype alongside the other four.

Inclusion and exclusion

Samples are dropped for the following reasons:

  1. Not a primary solid tumour — normal-adjacent samples leave the analysis cohort, but are kept aside as the comparison group.
  2. Duplicate aliquot from the same patient, collapsed to one.
  3. Missing survival time or vital status, for the survival analysis only.

Genes are filtered separately, and by a different rule at each stage: the differential-expression model keeps a gene with at least ten counts summed across all samples, while the co-expression network behind Results Figure 6 requires CPM ≥ 0.5 in at least 10% of tumours. Ten per cent of 1,095 is about 110 patients — roughly the size of the smallest intrinsic subtype. That is deliberate: a filter on the mean would discard a gene expressed in only one subtype, which in breast cancer is exactly the wrong thing to throw away.

Three places the numbers do not line up, on purpose.

Two de-duplication rules exist. The survival scripts keep the first aliquot per patient; the expression figures keep the aliquot with the largest library size. Eleven patients are affected and the two rules usually pick the same sample. Each script states its choice rather than one rule being imposed across the site.

Normalisation is not computed on the same matrix everywhere. The survival scripts run TMM on the de-duplicated tumour matrix of 1,095 columns; the expression figures run it on all 1,224 aliquots. A patient's log₂ CPM can therefore differ in the second decimal between two figures. Each figure is internally consistent; values are not interchangeable between them.

GSE70947 is 148/148 here and 147/146 in the figure. The series is fully paired at 296 samples, but the PPP4C panel drops the samples with a missing value for that one probe. The same two samples are why the Results page totals 1,570 tumour and 367 normal where this table sums to 1,571 and 369. Both numbers are right for their own context.
