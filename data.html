Section 02

Data

Where every number on this site comes from, which samples were removed before analysis, and how to get the same files yourself.

Datasets

TCGA-BRCA is the main cohort. It provides the expression data, clinical and follow-up records, somatic mutation calls and PAM50 subtype labels. The four GEO datasets are only used to check whether TCGA results hold up on other platforms and in other populations.

Why these four cohorts. I chose for platform diversity over size. If a gene shows up on Affymetrix and Agilent and an independent RNA-seq cohort, it's unlikely to be a probe artefact, whereas three studies on the same array platform could all share the same artefact. These four cover three platforms, ancestry from three continents, and both paired and unpaired designs. I looked at about thirty other GEO datasets and rejected them because they only had tumour samples, had unusable labels, or had too few normal samples to be useful.

Sample handling. GSE58135 includes 28 breast cancer cell lines, which I removed at download since this project compares tumour tissue with normal tissue. For GSE86374, I took the groups from the tissue description instead of the molecular-subtype field, because that field labels 14 of the tumours “Normal-like” and would have put them in the normal group. GSE70947 is the second cohort of a larger study, so its companion cohort can't be counted as separate replication later.

TCGA-BRCA cohort

The expression data has 60,660 Ensembl genes across 1,224 aliquots (individual samples): 1,111 primary tumours and 113 normal tissue samples. The tumours come from 1,095 patients, since 16 patients have more than one aliquot. All 113 normal samples are from patients who also have a tumour sample, which is what makes the matched-pair figure on the Results page possible.

Reported race

757 white · 183 Black or African American · 61 Asian · 1 American Indian or Alaska Native · 95 not reported. Most of the cohort is white, so the results may not apply equally to other groups. There's a gender column but it's empty, so I don't give a breakdown by sex anywhere on the site.

PAM50 subtypes, 1,083 of 1,095

Luminal A 562 (51.9%) · Luminal B 209 (19.3%) · Basal-like 190 (17.5%) · HER2-enriched 82 (7.6%) · Normal-like 40 (3.7%). Twelve patients have no subtype call, so I left them out instead of adding a sixth group.

Somatic mutations

89,568 masked somatic mutation calls across 991 samples. Fewer samples have mutation data than expression data, so the mutation cohort is a smaller subset and shouldn't be described using the expression n.

About the Normal-like group. Normal-like is usually thought to be an artefact of low tumour purity, meaning the sample had too little tumour in it to classify properly. I keep it labelled in every figure but don't treat it as a biological subtype like the other four.

Inclusion and exclusion

Samples were removed for these reasons:

  1. Not a primary solid tumour. Normal tissue samples are taken out of the analysis cohort but kept aside as the comparison group.
  2. Duplicate aliquot from the same patient, merged down to one.
  3. Missing survival time or vital status (survival analysis only).

Genes were filtered separately, using a different rule at each stage. For differential expression, a gene was kept if it had at least ten counts summed across all samples. For the co-expression network behind Results Figure 6, a gene needed CPM ≥ 0.5 in at least 10% of tumours. 10% of 1,095 is about 110 patients, which is roughly the size of the smallest subtype. I set it that way deliberately. A filter based on average expression would drop genes that are only expressed in one subtype, and in breast cancer those are some of the most important genes to keep.

Why some numbers differ between figures

Two de-duplication rules. The survival scripts keep the first aliquot for each patient, and the expression figures keep the one with the largest library size. This affects eleven patients, and both rules usually pick the same sample. Each script states which rule it uses.

Normalisation. The survival scripts run TMM normalisation on the de-duplicated tumour data (1,095 samples), while the expression figures run it on all 1,224 aliquots. As a result, a patient's log₂ CPM can differ in the second decimal place between figures. Each figure is internally consistent, but values shouldn't be compared across them.

GSE70947 counts. This table says 148/148 and the figure says 147/146. The dataset is fully paired at 296 samples, but the PPP4C panel drops samples with a missing value for that probe. Those dropped samples are also why the Results page totals 1,570 tumour and 367 normal while this table adds up to 1,571 and 369. Both numbers are correct for what they describe.
