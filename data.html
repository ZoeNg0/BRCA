Section 02

Data

Where the data came from, which patient groups and samples were included, and how samples and genes were filtered and counted before analysis.

Datasets used

The main TCGA dataset and the four GEO datasets used to replicate its results.

TCGA-BRCA was the main dataset. It provided the gene expression data, clinical and follow-up information, mutation data and PAM50 subtype labels. Four datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), a public repository of gene expression studies, were used only for replication: to check whether the tumour-versus-normal results from TCGA could be repeated in different patients and on different measurement platforms.

Sample counts were checked against the metadata for each sample, not taken from paper abstracts.

Why these four datasets were chosen. I prioritised a range of measurement platforms over sample size. A result that appears on Affymetrix arrays, Agilent arrays and an independent RNA-seq dataset is unlikely to be caused by a quirk of one technology, while three studies on the same platform could share the same technical bias. Together, these four datasets cover three platforms, patients with ancestry from three continents, and both paired and unpaired designs. I reviewed about thirty other GEO datasets and excluded them because they had no normal samples, had unclear sample labels, or had too few normal samples to be useful.

Dataset-specific decisions. GSE58135 also contains 28 breast cancer cell lines, which were removed because this project compares tumour tissue with normal tissue. In GSE86374, tumour and normal groups were taken from the tissue description instead of the molecular-subtype field, because that field labels 14 tumours “Normal-like” and would have placed them in the normal group. GSE70947 is the second cohort of a larger study, so its companion cohort can't later be used as additional, independent replication.

TCGA-BRCA cohort

The samples, clinical characteristics, subtypes and mutation data in the TCGA cohort.

The TCGA expression data covers 60,660 genes (Ensembl IDs) across 1,224 aliquots, which are individual samples taken from a tissue specimen. Of these, 1,111 are primary tumours and 113 are normal breast tissue. The tumour samples come from 1,095 patients, because 16 patients have more than one aliquot. All 113 normal samples come from patients who also have a tumour sample, which made it possible to compare tumour and normal tissue within the same person.

757 white · 183 Black or African American · 61 Asian · 1 American Indian or Alaska Native · 95 not reported. Most patients in the cohort are white, so the results may not apply equally to other populations. The dataset's gender column is empty, so no breakdown by sex is given on this site.

PAM50 subtypes, 1,083 of 1,095

Luminal A 562 (51.9%) · Luminal B 209 (19.3%) · Basal-like 190 (17.5%) · HER2-enriched 82 (7.6%) · Normal-like 40 (3.7%). Twelve patients have no subtype call and were excluded from subtype analyses instead of being shown as a separate group.

Somatic mutations

89,568 somatic mutation calls across 991 samples, using the GDC's “masked” files that are filtered for open-access release. Fewer samples have mutation data than expression data, so the mutation analysis has its own, smaller sample size.

The Normal-like group. Normal-like tumours are generally thought to reflect samples with low tumour content, where too much normal tissue is present for the tumour to be classified properly. This group is kept and labelled in every figure but isn't treated as a distinct biological subtype.

Sample and gene filtering

The rules used to exclude samples and genes, and why some numbers differ between figures.

Samples were excluded for the following reasons:

  1. Not a primary tumour. Normal tissue samples were kept separately as the comparison group.
  2. Duplicate aliquots from the same patient, reduced to one per patient.
  3. Missing survival time or vital status (survival analysis only).

Genes were filtered separately, using a different rule for each analysis. For differential expression, a gene needed at least ten reads in total across all samples. For the co-expression network behind Results Figure 6, a gene needed a CPM (counts per million, a measure that adjusts for sequencing depth) of at least 0.5 in at least 10% of tumours. Ten percent of 1,095 tumours is about 110 patients, roughly the size of the smallest subtype. This threshold was chosen so that genes expressed mainly in one subtype would still be kept, since a filter based on average expression across all tumours could remove them.

Why some numbers differ between figures

De-duplication. The survival analysis keeps the first aliquot for each patient, while the expression figures keep the aliquot with the largest library size (the most sequencing reads). This affects eleven patients, and the two rules usually choose the same sample.

Normalisation. The survival analysis applies TMM normalisation to the de-duplicated tumour data (1,095 samples), while the expression figures apply it to all 1,224 aliquots. Because of this, a patient's log₂ CPM value can differ slightly (in the second decimal place) between figures. Each figure is internally consistent, but values shouldn't be compared directly between them.

GSE70947 sample counts. The table above lists 148 tumour and 148 normal samples, but the figure shows 147 and 146. The dataset is fully paired at 296 samples, but the PPP4C panel leaves out samples with a missing value for the PPP4C probe. These missing values are also why the Results page totals 1,570 tumour and 367 normal samples, while the table above adds up to 1,571 and 369.
