Background

Enough biology to make the rest of the site readable by someone who has taken one year of high-school genetics.

Why breast cancer

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer worldwide. It is also one of the best-characterised at the molecular level, which makes it an unusually good subject for a student project: the public data is large, well annotated, and there are decades of published findings to check your own results against. If an analysis recovers something already known, that is evidence the pipeline works, not a wasted effort.

The intrinsic molecular subtypes

Sorting tumours by which genes they switch on, rather than by how they look under a microscope, produces a small number of recurring groups. The standard scheme recognises five, usually assigned with a 50-gene classifier called PAM50.

What gene expression data is

Every cell carries the same DNA but transcribes different parts of it into RNA. RNA sequencing counts those transcripts, so a tumour becomes a vector of roughly twenty thousand numbers: one per gene, each an estimate of how actively that gene was being read at the moment the sample was frozen.

Three consequences shape everything downstream. Counts depend on how deeply a sample was sequenced, so they must be normalised before samples can be compared. Counts are noisy for lowly-expressed genes, so weak genes are filtered out. And testing twenty thousand genes at once produces false positives by the hundred, so p-values must be corrected for multiple testing.

What TCGA is

The Cancer Genome Atlas is a public archive of molecular and clinical data from more than eleven thousand tumours across thirty-three cancer types. The breast cancer cohort, TCGA-BRCA, pairs expression data with de-identified clinical records including stage, receptor status and follow-up time. That pairing is what makes survival analysis possible from a laptop.

ⓘ

All data used here is open-access and de-identified. No controlled-access or individually identifiable data is involved. See Data › access and ethics.

Where this project fits

Nothing in this project is novel to the field: the subtypes are established and the best-known prognostic genes have been reported many times over. The aim is to reproduce that structure independently, from raw public data, using a documented pipeline, and to see how far a careful re-analysis gets.
