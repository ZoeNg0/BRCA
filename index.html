PPP4C expression in breast cancer

This project uses public RNA sequencing data from 1,111 primary breast tumours in The Cancer Genome Atlas, plus four independent cohorts, to test whether PPP4C (the catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 4) is raised in tumours, differs between subtypes, and tracks with how long patients survive.

The question

Research question

Is PPP4C expression raised in breast tumours, does it differ between the intrinsic molecular subtypes, and does it predict patient survival independently of stage and age?

Breast cancer is really several diseases. Tumours that look the same under a microscope can behave very differently, and since the early 2000s a lot of that has been explained by gene expression, which divides tumours into intrinsic subtypes with different treatments and outcomes. Public databases now have expression data for thousands of tumours, so a student with a laptop can go back to questions that used to need a lab.

PPP4C is the catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 4, an enzyme complex involved in DNA damage repair and the cell cycle. It's been reported as over-expressed in several solid tumours, so I wanted to test it independently in a large public breast cancer cohort.

The project has four aims:

  1. Characterise the cohort: match each sample (aliquot) back to its patient, and work out which sample count belongs to which question.
  2. Quantify PPP4C in tumours compared with nearby normal tissue, and across the five PAM50 subtypes.
  3. Connect PPP4C to patient outcome with survival analysis, and check whether the link holds after accounting for stage, age and subtype.
  4. Contextualise it by building a co-expression network from the tumour-versus-normal signal, and asking which biological programme that network is built around.

Main finding

PPP4C is higher in breast tumours in all five cohorts and in 109 of 113 matched patients. It's raised in every subtype, and patients with high PPP4C had worse five-year survival, although that result is only borderline significant after adjusting for stage and subtype.

Process

Project steps

  1. Choosing the gene

    I picked one target gene at the start instead of searching every gene for a hit, and read the published work on the PP4 complex in cancer so I knew which results would agree with it and which wouldn't.

  2. Downloading and checking TCGA-BRCA

    Downloaded expression counts, clinical records and somatic mutation calls from the GDC. Matched 1,224 aliquots to 1,095 patients and kept the 113 patients with a matched normal sample as a separate control group.

  3. Differential expression

    Ran a negative-binomial model across all genes, set up so a positive fold change means higher in tumour, with thresholds chosen before looking at the results.

  4. Replication in four cohorts

    Added four GEO cohorts from three other platforms, analysed each with the method suited to its data type, and combined all five by rank aggregation instead of intersecting gene lists.

  5. Subtype and survival analysis

    Compared PPP4C across the five PAM50 subtypes, then ran Kaplan-Meier curves and a Cox model adjusted for stage, age and subtype. I checked the proportional-hazards assumption instead of assuming it held.

  6. Co-expression network

    Built a network linking genes whose expression moves together across the cohort, then tested proliferation and motility gene sets against the most connected genes. Sets that failed are reported along with the ones that passed.

  7. Write-up

    Compared each finding with the published work, wrote up the limitations, and built this site so every figure shows its sample count, test and caveats.
