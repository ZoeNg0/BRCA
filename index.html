PPP4C in breast cancer, read from public tumour transcriptomes

A computational analysis of RNA sequencing data from 1,111 primary breast tumours in The Cancer Genome Atlas, with replication in four independent cohorts, asking whether PPP4C — the catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 4 — is elevated in tumours, whether it differs between the molecular subtypes, and whether it tracks with how long patients survive.

The question

What this project asks

Is PPP4C expression raised in breast tumours, does it differ between the intrinsic molecular subtypes, and does it predict patient survival independently of stage and age?

Breast cancer is not one disease. Tumours that look similar under a microscope can behave completely differently, and since the early 2000s that difference has been explained largely by gene expression: distinct intrinsic subtypes with distinct treatments and outcomes. Public repositories now hold expression measurements for thousands of tumours, which means a student with a laptop can re-ask questions that once needed a laboratory.

PPP4C is the catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 4, an enzyme complex involved in DNA damage repair and cell cycle progression. It has been reported as over-expressed in several solid tumours, which makes it a reasonable target to re-examine independently in a large public breast cancer cohort.

It breaks into four aims:

  1. Characterise the cohort — resolve aliquots to patients, and establish which sample count honestly belongs to which question.
  2. Quantify PPP4C expression in tumours against adjacent normal tissue, and across the five PAM50 subtypes.
  3. Connect PPP4C to clinical outcome with survival analysis, and check whether the association survives adjustment for stage, age and subtype.
  4. Contextualise it by building a co-expression network from the tumour-versus-normal signal and asking what programme that network is organised around.

In one sentence

PPP4C is raised in breast tumours in all five cohorts and in 109 of 113 matched patients, is raised in every intrinsic subtype rather than confined to one, and marks worse five-year survival — though adjusting for stage and subtype leaves that last result at the edge of significance.

How it ran

The work, in the order it happened

  1. Scope the question and read the literature

    Settle on a single pre-chosen target gene rather than a fishing expedition, and collect the published work on the PP4 complex in cancer to know what result would agree with it and what would not.

  2. Acquire and quality-check TCGA-BRCA

    Pull expression counts, clinical records and somatic mutation calls from the GDC; resolve 1,224 aliquots down to 1,095 patients, and keep the 113 with a matched normal sample aside as their own control group.

  3. Differential expression, tumour against normal

    A negative-binomial model across the transcriptome, with the reference level set so a positive fold change means up in tumour, and thresholds fixed in advance.

  4. Replicate in four independent cohorts

    Assemble four GEO cohorts on three further platforms, run the differential expression method each one's measurement actually calls for, and combine all five by rank aggregation rather than by intersecting gene lists.

  5. Subtype and survival

    Read PPP4C across the five PAM50 subtypes, then Kaplan-Meier curves and a Cox model adjusted for stage, age and subtype, with the proportional-hazards assumption checked rather than assumed.

  6. Build the co-expression network

    Ask what programme the tumour signal is organised around by connecting genes that move together across the cohort, then test proliferation and motility gene sets against its most connected genes — reporting the sets that fail alongside the ones that pass.

  7. Write it up

    Compare every finding against the published work, state the limitations in full, and build this site so each figure carries its own sample count, test and caveats.
